# Sculpting with a Mask

> **Note:** This document was generated by Claude Code and has not been reviewed by a human. Please use it as a reference only.

A **Mask** protects part of the mesh from other sculpt brushes, so you can edit one area — like the eyes and mouth of this face — without disturbing the rest.

## Step 1: Paint a Mask

Select **Mask** from the brush shelf (or press `3`).

![Mask brush highlighted, with a tooltip showing its shortcut](img/sculpt-with-mask-01.png)

Drag over the areas you want to protect.

![Eyes and mouth areas painted with a mask, shown as a darker overlay](img/sculpt-with-mask-02.png)

## Step 2: Invert the Mask

Press `Ctrl+I` to invert the mask, so the areas you just painted become editable and everything else becomes protected instead.

![Mask inverted, with "Ctrl + I" labeled](img/sculpt-with-mask-03.png)

## Step 3: Sculpt Within the Unmasked Area

Select **Clay Strips** again.

![Clay Strips brush highlighted, with a tooltip describing it](img/sculpt-with-mask-04.png)

Hold `Ctrl` while dragging to carve inward instead of building up material. The mask keeps the surrounding face untouched.

![Ctrl + Drag labeled, with the eye sockets and mouth now carved inward](img/sculpt-with-mask-05.png)

## Step 4: Remesh for Clean Topology

Open the **Remesh** panel and set **Voxel Size**, then click **Remesh** to rebuild the mesh with clean, evenly distributed geometry.

![Remesh panel open with Voxel Size set to 0.1 m](img/sculpt-with-mask-06.png)

![Voxel Size field highlighted, being set to a finer 0.01 m](img/sculpt-with-mask-07.png)

![Face after remeshing, with a much more even vertex distribution](img/sculpt-with-mask-08.png)

## Step 5: Smooth the Result

Select **Smooth**, increase its **Size**, then drag over any remaining rough areas.

![Smooth brush highlighted, with a tooltip describing it](img/sculpt-with-mask-09.png)

![Final smoothed face with softened eye sockets and mouth groove](img/sculpt-with-mask-10.png)
